Figure 1 | Construction of an articulated elephantnose fish model from a textured surface mesh.

a, Supplied external surface model of Gnathonemus petersii, normalized to a full model length of 200 mm. b, Curved body interfaces obtained by spherical segmentation, with the chin socket indicated. Blue curves show projected boundaries between the outer surface and the spherical cut faces on the camera-facing side. c, Exploded assembly of 14 body regions, seven whole fin meshes and one independent chin segment. The seven fins comprise dorsal, anal and caudal fins, and paired pectoral and pelvic fins. Colours distinguish body regions, fins and chin; separation distances are illustrative. d, Reassembled model with projected joint centres and parent–child connections, comprising a floating root and 40 actuated rotational degrees of freedom. The connections depict the implemented kinematic tree, not reconstructed bones. e, Textured rigid-body assembly in MuJoCo. Original surface UV coordinates are retained where the source surface is unchanged; new ball surfaces receive projected texture coordinates. f, Enlarged view of the rigid sensory chin and its concentric spherical attachment, with a ball radius of 2.41 mm. Configured yaw, pitch and roll ranges are ±45°, ±45° and ±30°, respectively; these are engineering settings, not measured anatomical limits. All panels show the rest pose. Scale bars: 20 mm (a, e) and 5 mm (f). The input is a supplied external mesh, not a micro-CT reconstruction.
